Making Decisions

PROJECT FILES

  • Course: Making Decisions
  • Planning Docs: Outline ,   Visual Storyboard

THE CHALLENGE

New managers often view complex problems from a single perspective, such as how a situation might affect the bottom line. This eLearning course helps managers learn how to apply Bolman & Deal's 4 Frames model when making decisions in order to avoid single-perspective thinking.

MY APPROACH

Storytelling can be an effective way to help learners adopt a new way of thinking. My approach was to allow learners to "look under the hood" of leadership decision making by following two leaders as they engage in two common scenarios: taking over for an outgoing boss, and making a promotion decision between two qualified candidates.

DESIGN RATIONALE

An eLearning course allows learners to record reflections as they witness decision making in action, and complete low stakes knowledge checks as a way to engage more deeply with the four frames. The course sets the stage for future conversations with supervisors about context-specific decision making.

CS for Oregon Promo Video

PROJECT FILES

  • Video: ECS Promo
  • Planning Documents: Written Storyboard

THE CHALLENGE

Computer Science for Oregon is a collaboration between Portland State University, University of Oregon, and Oregon State University Cascades. The team wanted a creative way to spread the word about their summer workshop for high school teachers.

MY APPROACH

Given that visual information sometimes captures attention more than text, I decided to create a promotional video for the summer workshop. The team showcased this video in a follow-up email after the initial text-based invitation.

DESIGN RATIONALE

The Exploring Computer Science Program allows teachers certified in any subject to build the necessary skills to teach a foundational program for high school students. As the video starts, you meet four teachers who successfully completed the program and learn about their diverse backgrounds (journalism, software engineer, art teacher and elementary teacher). Next you see a map indicating the reach of the program across the state, followed by additional details about the program.

Properly Packed: The Art & Science of Bagging Groceries

PROJECT FILES

  • Challenge Info
  • View Course
  • Planning Docs: Visual Outline , Written Storyboard

THE CHALLENGE

I created this course as part of Tim Slade's March 2025 eLearning Challenge. The goal was to improve customer satisfaction scores linked to their checkout experience.

MY APPROACH

While doing research, I discovered a news story about a previous winner of the national Best Bagger competition mentoring a new contestant. Rather than focus on poor customer service scores, this approach invites employees to join the competition to better their skills.

DESIGN RATIONALE

I used an inquiry-based “spot the error” activity where learners take notes on an improperly packed bag, then compare their notes with Anita’s annotations. This approach honors the learner's experience (Knowles & Merrill) and provides a kind of demonstration (Merrill) to build knowledge. I also include a sorting activity where learners have to choose categories which have implications for proper packing, such as chilled or crushable items. This simulation sets the stage for hands-on practice with a mentor.

CidiLabs Design Tools: How-To Video

PROJECT FILES

  • Designing Pages with Cidilabs

THE CHALLENGE

High school teachers were expected to use a common tool for class webpages. In order to provide more design features, the district introduced Cidi Labs Design tools. Teachers needed "just enough, just in time" support to use this tool for course development.

MY APPROACH

While the vendor provided comprehensive instructions for using their tool, a quick video tutorial allows teachers to see the tool in their context. In addition, the custom video focused only on the steps needed to achieve success.

DESIGN RATIONALE

A screencast tutorial provided enough scaffolding for users to get started with this new software tool. Users could speed up, slow down, and stop the demonstration as needed to accomplish their goals.
